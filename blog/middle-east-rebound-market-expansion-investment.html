The Middle East is set to rebound. Expansion and investment are the way forward.

By the team at Soliton Power

2026 has been one of the hardest years the Gulf has faced in decades. But behind the headlines, the region’s economies have held together, and the outlook for 2027 is strong. For business owners, the lesson of this year is clear: don’t depend on one market, one route or one customer base.

A hard year, and a strong forecast

The regional conflict, and the disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, hit oil exports, trade, travel and investor confidence across the region. In its latest regional outlook, published this month, the International Monetary Fund now expects GCC economies to shrink by 4.8% in 2026, down from the growth it had forecast in April.

The same outlook expects a sharp rebound of 9.1% in 2027, as oil and gas exports return to normal. Those forecasts assume the conflict gradually ends by early 2027, so the timing could move, but the direction is clear. Other forecasters, including ICAEW, also expect a strong recovery next year.

The IMF also pointed to what kept the region standing: years of investment in infrastructure, strong government finances, and reforms such as Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030. The Gulf has not stopped investing either. Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE committed about $120 billion to AI in 2025 alone.

Why expansion is the path forward

When a single route or market can be disrupted overnight, the safest businesses are the ones with more than one. Gulf governments are acting on this lesson. Regional economists have named diversifying trade routes and markets as a central priority for the recovery, and the UAE is widening its network of trade agreements well beyond its traditional partners.

Canada is one of the clearest examples. On October 27, Canada and the UAE signed a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement, negotiated in a record 47 days, along with a new strategic partnership between the two countries. Canada has also set a goal of doubling its exports to markets outside the United States by 2035. Both sides want more trade and more investment with each other.

What this means for your business

If you run a company in the Gulf or the wider Middle East and North Africa, now is the time to plan your next market, not after the recovery arrives. North America offers:

  • Stable demand. Two large, wealthy consumer and business markets with predictable rules.
  • A gateway in Canada. A new trade agreement with the UAE, strong ties to the US market, and a government actively seeking partners outside the US.
  • Room to invest. Businesses, real estate and partnerships that can balance a portfolio concentrated in one region.
  • Talent and technology. Engineering, AI and software talent, especially in cities like Vancouver and Toronto.
